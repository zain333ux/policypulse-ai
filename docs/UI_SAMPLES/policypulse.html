Plain-language summary

Amendment 12-b rewrites the city's residential parking permit process. It raises permit fees by 18%, shortens the renewal window from 60 to 30 days, and removes the option to apply in person at neighborhood branch libraries, centralizing all applications at city hall. It also adds a low-income fee waiver, though the waiver's application process is not yet defined.

§4.2 — Application method

Applications must be submitted in person at city hall during regular business hours online, by mail, or in person at city hall or any branch library, within 30 days of the renewal notice.

§4.3 — Renewal window

Permit renewal applications are due 30 45 days before the current permit expires, with a 15-day grace period for late renewals.

The low-income fee waiver application process will be determined by city staff is defined in Appendix C and published on the city website no later than 30 days before this amendment takes effect.

Executive memo

ToCity council, district planning committee FromOffice of policy analysis DateJune 17, 2026 RePublic feedback analysis — Amendment 12-b

Of the 1,247 public comments reviewed, 62% expressed support for Amendment 12-b, largely citing the new low-income fee waiver as an important equity measure. Opposition and mixed reactions concentrated almost entirely on two provisions: the removal of in-person branch applications, cited by 342 respondents, and the shortened 30-day renewal window, raised by 210.

The gap analysis in this report maps each concern to the specific section it affects. In each case, a targeted revision addresses the concern without altering the amendment's core purpose or revenue assumptions.

We recommend the council adopt the three revisions documented in this report: restoring branch-office and online submission as valid application methods, extending the renewal window to 45 days with a 15-day grace period, and committing to publish the fee-waiver process no later than 30 days before the amendment takes effect.
